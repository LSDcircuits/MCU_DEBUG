General Information
This exercise schedule is based on ISA and applicable up to and including 3000 ft. For other
situations consult the AFM. Chapter numbers correspond to the flight training exercise numbers
as defined in EASA Part-FCL.
6 Straight and level
Straight and level flight
1. Look out – select an outside reference point
2. Pitch for horizontal flight
3. Speed 110 KIAS (±65% power setting)
4. Trim as required
5. Maintain wings level using ailerons
6. Maintain coordinated flight using rudder (ball
centered)
7. Look out and check:
• Fuel quantity and fuel balance
• Radio
• Engine instruments
• Direction (outside reference point)
• Altitude
7 Climbing
Transition to climb (80 KIAS)
1. Look out – scan for traffic and select an out-
side reference point
2. Check engine instruments
3. Pitch for climb attitude (80 KIAS)
4. Approaching 80 KIAS, set full power (100%)
5. Maintain coordinated flight using rudder (ball
centered)
6. Trim as required
7. Look out and check:
• Pitch attitude
• Direction, wings level
• Airspeed
Level-off from climb (110 KIAS)
1.±20 ft before target altitude, set pitch for hor-
izontal flight
2. Maintain wings level using ailerons
3. Maintain coordinated flight using rudder (ball
centered)
4. Approaching 110 KIAS,±65% power
5. Trim as required
8 Descending
Transition to cruise descent (110 KIAS)
1. Reduce power to±20% (standard descent
500 ft/min)
2. Pitch to maintain 110 KIAS
3. Trim as required
4. Maintain wings level using ailerons
5. Maintain coordinated flight using rudder (ball
centered)
6. Look out and check:
• Pitch attitude
• Direction, wings level
• Airspeed
Level-off from cruise descent
1.±20 ft before the target altitude, increase
power to±65%
2. Pitch for horizontal flight
3. Trim as required
4. Maintain wings level using ailerons
5. Maintain coordinated flight using rudder (ball
centered)
6. Speed 110 KIAS (±65% power)

9 Turning
Normal coordinated turn (30◦ bank)
1. Look out – scan for traffic and select an out-
side reference point
2. Roll into 30 ◦ bank, use rudder to counteract
adverse yaw
3. Look out and check:
• Bank / slip indicator
• Altitude / pitch
4. Roll out, use rudder to counteract adverse
yaw
Climbing turn
1. Look out, check engine instruments
2. Establish normal climb (as explained in Exer-
cise 7)
3. Max. bank 20 ◦, use rudder to counteract ad-
verse yaw
4. Look out and check:
• Bank / slip indicator
• Speed / pitch
5. Roll out, stay coordinated
6. Level-off (as explained in Exercise 7)
Descending turn
1. Look out
2. Establish cruise descent (as explained in
Chapter 8, power±25%)
3. Bank 30 ◦, use rudder to counteract adverse
yaw
4. Look out and check:
• Bank / slip indicator
• Speed / pitch
5. Look out, roll out, stay coordinated
6. Level-off or continue descent (as explained in
Chapter 8)

10B Stalling
Crew briefing preceding the stall
• Type of stall
• Altitude to maintain and regain
• Direction or heading to maintain
Recovery of the stall is started at:
Full stall
Nose and/or wing drop or (excessive) rate
of descent with full back pressure
Approach to stall landing configuration
Whichever comes first: stall warning, 5
KIAS above the bottom white arc with
flaps landing, or the buffet
Approach to stall clean
Whichever comes first: stall warning, 5
KIAS above the bottom of the green arc
with flaps landing, or the buffet
Pre-stall checklist
Inside inspection (before every stall, also
appr. to the stall):
1.Trim . . . . . . . Checked (trimmed for level flight)
2.Flaps . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .Retracted (UP)
3.Engine . Checked (instruments in green, fuel
balanced)
4.Security Checked (harnesses tight, baggage
stowed)
Outside inspection (before every stall, also
appr. to the stall):
1.Altitude . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . Checked
2.Position . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . Checked
3.Orientation . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . Checked
4.Sky . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . Free
Altitude:Recovered before:
•Full Stall:Dual 2000 ft AGL, Solo 3000 ft
AGL
•Approach to the Stall:Dual 1500 ft AGL,
Solo 2500 ft AGL
Position:Never stall above:
• Built up areas
• Harbours and industrial areas
• Populated areas
• Airports / CTRs
• Extended water areas
• 4/8 clouds or more
• Other traffic, etc.
Orientation:Choose an outside reference point
Sky:Make 90 ◦ right and left clearing turns or a
left hand 180◦ turn to check the airspace ahead
and below for other traffic
Various stalls
Full stall without flaps selected (clean)
1. Look out – scan for traffic and select an out-
side reference point
2. Power idle
3. Maintain altitude, don’t use the trim
4. Maintain coordinated flight
5. Move the stick progressively backwards until
the stall
Full stall recovery with power (clean)
1. Pitch down to reduce the angle of attack, si-
multaneously call “RECOVER”
2. Check for increasing speed (± 70 KIAS)
3. Pitch up gently while moving the power lever
gently forward to reach full power when the
nose is passing through the horizon
4. Maintain coordinated flight using rudder (ball
centered)
5. Pitch for climb attitude (80 KIAS)
6. Climb back to original altitude
7. Speed 110 KIAS (± 65%)
Full stall recovery without power (clean)
1. Pitch down to reduce the angle of attack, si-
multaneously call “RECOVER”
2. Check for increasing speed (± 70 KIAS)
3. Maintain coordinated flight using rudder (ball
centered)
4. Adjust pitch for glide attitude (75 KIAS), trim
5. When established in the glide, end the exer-
cise and climb back to original altitude
6. Speed 110 KIAS (± 65%)

Wing dip recovery
1. Power idle, ailerons neutral
2. Call “Recover” simultaneous pitch down be-
low glide attitude to break the stall
3. Full opposite rudder with feet
4. Neutralize the rudder with feet when rotation
stops
5. Check for increasing speed (±80 KIAS)
6. Pull gently out of the dive, while moving the
power lever gently forward, to reach full power
when the nose is passing through the horizon.
7. Direction with feet, no bank
8. Adjust pitch for climb attitude (80 KIAS)
9. Climb back to previous altitude
Emergency recovery
Just in case you don’t know the procedure any-
more:
1. Power idle
2. Ailerons and elevator neutral
3. Apply full opposite rudder against rotation
4. Neutralize rudder
5. Pull gently out of the dive, wings level
6. Full power when nose is passing the horizon
7. Climb back to desired altitude
This general procedure is applicable for all
JAR23 & FAR23 approved aircraft, provided
mass & balance falls in the envelope and the
aircraft is trimmed for cruise flight. Consult the
AFM/Diamond for the DA40 specific procedure.
12 Take-off and climb
Normal take-off
1. Confirm correct runway from signage / mark-
ings. Call out: “Runway [number], identified”
2. Scan the approach sector and the full length
of the runway
Call out: “Approach sector and runway clear”
3. Line-up items:
• Parking brakes released
• Transponder ALT
• Landing light on when clearance received
• Pitot heat ON (in visible moisture or if OAT
is below +5 ◦C)
4. Line up on the runway centerline
5. Check runway direction
Call out: “Runway heading verified”
6. Check the windsock, ailerons into the wind
7. Heels on the floor, look down the end of the
runway
8. Set full power (100%)
9. Maintain runway centreline using the rudder
10. Check CED — power 100% / 2300 RPM / all
indications green
Call out: “Take-off power set”
11. Confirm the airspeed indicator is reading
Call out: “Airspeed alive”
12. Reduce ailerons input when accelerating/
keep glare shield parallel to horizon
13. Rotate at 60 KIAS (Vr)
14. Pitch for climb speed 70 KIAS
15. Maintain runway track using ailerons
16. Maintain coordination using the rudder (ball
centered)
17. Passing 200 ft AGL select flaps up
18. Set pitch for climb 80 KIAS
Crosswind take-off

inputs
5. Once airborne make a coordinated turn into
the wind
6. Maintain runway track using ailerons
7. Correct for increasing crosswind during the
climb
8. Proceed as normal take off (items 13-19).

13 Circuit, approach and landing
Normal circuit and approach
1. Climb to circuit altitude and level off
2. Speed 90 KIAS, power±45%
3. Look out and turn to crosswind, 30◦ bank
4. Climbing turn after passing 200 ft is permitted
to stay in the circuit area (climbing turn max
20◦ bank)
5. Look out and turn to downwind, 30◦ bank
6. Downwind checks (FRESBL):
•F — Flaps:Check speed, call “SPEED
CHECKED”→select flaps take-off
•R — Radio:Downwind call (e.g., PH-RAB
downwind runway 06)
•E — Engine:Instruments in green, fuel
quantity & balance checked
•S — Security:Harnesses secure, loose
items stowed
•B — Brakes:Check pressure, heels on
floor
•L — Lights:Landing light ON
7. Turn to base leg when runway is 45◦ behind
8. Reduce power to±15%
9. Maintain altitude until 80 KIAS, start descent
10. Trim as required, PID
11. Turn to final, maximum 30◦ of bank
12. On final: flaps LDG, 70 KIAS, trimmed (adjust
power as required to maintain glide path)
13. Establish on stabilized glide path
Normal landing
1. Fly standard circuit
2. On final: flaps landing
3. 70 KIAS final approach speed
4. Trim as required
5. Aim for the aiming point
6. Close the power lever gently
7. Start horizontal flight just before touchdown,
and shift vision from the aiming point to the
end of the runway or beyond using peripheral
vision
8. Maintain directional control with rudder
9. Maintain runway centreline (lateral move-
ment) using ailerons
10. Increase pitch attitude, maintain level flight
(flare)
11. Touchdown on main wheels

1. Call out: “Aborting!”
2. Close the power immediately
3. Maintain runway centreline using rudder
4. Apply brakes as necessary
5. Elevator neutral / aileron into the wind
6. RT call: “call sign, rejected take-off”
7. Vacate the runway as soon as practical

(SEFATO)

Glide in (from circuit altitude)
1. Fly standard circuit (items 1-6), but
2. No flaps on downwind, speed 90 KIAS
3. Maintain circuit altitude on downwind and/or

4. Maintain speed 90 KIAS
5. Close power lever when landing is assured

9. Final checks
10. Plan full flaps landing, touch down at 60 KIAS

Energy Management Note
Judge the power closed point based on wind
direction and speed to reach the field safely.
S-turns and side-slipping mustonlybe used
as a last resort to lose excess height, and not
as a primary method to correct the glide path.
Flapless landing
1. Fly standard circuit
2. Maintain 90 KIAS on downwind
3. Speed 85 KIAS on base leg
4. Establish on stabilized glide path
5. Power / attitude 75 KIAS on final
6. Nose attitude higher than for normal landing
7. Aim for the aiming point
8. Close power lever gently
9. Do not flare out, maintain a slightly higher at-
titude during touchdown.
10. Proceed as normal landing (items 12-16).
Wave-off
1. Wave-off is a go-around below 50 ft AGL
2. Don’t attempt to make turns at low altitude
3. Due to aircraft inertia, the main wheels may
briefly touch the runway (do not pull back ex-
cessively to prevent this, and do not deliber-
ately land)
4. Proceed as go-around

2. Speed 110 KIAS

Spiral dive entry (with FI only)
1. Start a steep turn with 45 degrees of bank
2. Lower the nose gently below the horizon

16 Forced landing
Forced landing procedure
1. Maintain altitude till speed bleeds off to 75
KIAS and start looking for a suitable field
2. Pitch for glide attitude and trim for 75 KIAS
3. Depending on altitude/position turn downwind
and select a landing area, below 1200 ft turn
direct base leg for the most suitable landing
area.
4. Perform abbreviated troubleshoot
5. Select the most suitable landing area within
reach and use to the 1000 ft point
6. May Day call / transponder on 7700/ ELT Man.
(T) (when time permitting)
7. Proceed to the 1000 ft point
8. Perform expanded trouble shoot if possible,
perform the restart procedure
9. After passing the 1000 ft point turn to base leg
10. On final aim at 1/3 of the field to guarantee
making the field
11. flaps T/O when landing assured
12. Adjust attitude for 70 KIAS
13. Plan full flaps landing 60 KIAS
14. When landing is assured, shift aiming point to
the beginning of runway or field

17 Precautionary landing
Consider a precautionary landing when:
• Weather deteriorates below VMC minima
• After UDP
• Other circumstance affecting flight safety that
require a precautionary landing
Aircraft should be technically in a good condi-
tion!
Precautionary landing procedure
1. Perform downwind checks and select flaps
T/O
2. Fly down the wind
3. PAN PAN call / transponder on 7700/ ELT man
(T)
4. Position on downwind of the suitable field
5. Fly a close circuit at 500 ft AGL
6. Be aware of high obstacles
7. Make a descending turn towards the selected
area and carry out an inspection run at 200 ft
AGL Check the condition of the landing area
8. Check the compass direction in poor visibility
9. Aim for a landmark in good visibility
10. Climb to 500 ft AGL, flaps up on the up-wind
leg

Requirements for a landing field:
• No obstacles on final (or upwind)
• Long enough (min. 300 m)
• Headwind (or light crosswind)
• Flat
• Preference for (short) grassland over plowed
land
